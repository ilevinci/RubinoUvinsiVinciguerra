\section{INTRODUCTION}
\subsection{Purpose}
The increasing global focus and effort on sustainable mobility and the rising popularity of cycling both as a sport and as a way to navigate around cities have risen new challenges in the everyday life of millions of people. Even if governments are beginning to address the need for new infrastructure for cyclists, the transition is complex and slow and the current infrastructure is often insufficient and outdated. Cyclists often find themselves having to face the danger of unexpected obstacles on their route and of worn-out paths which require maintenance to ensure both safety and navigational efficiency for their daily commutes and training sessions. 
In order for this transition to happen, local institutions require a structured and scalable mechanism to systematically collect, consolidate and share data about the conditions of paths. This data has to come from the direct experience of a vast and distributed community of cyclists by offering them a platform that is useful and appealing for them, regardless of whether they cycle to train or to commute. \\
The purpose of Best Bike Paths (BPP) is to provide a comprehensive software platform that is useful for cyclists as it offers an integrated environment where they can track their session and signal criticalities of paths by therefore contributing to the creation and maintenance of a reliable inventory of bike paths, that also local institutions can benefit from.

\subsubsection{Goals}
\begin{itemize}
    \item G1: Bikers share a path that they have ridden along with other bikers. 
    \item G2: Bikers give other bikers information about the status and the presence of obstacles on paths that they have ridden along. 
    \item G3: Bikers are able to track and record their cycling trips to maintain a log of their activity that contains relevant information about their performance. 
    \item G4: Bikers are presented with a set of paths, comprehensive of information about the path's status and navigational efficiency, between a specified origin and destination. 
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Scope}
The core function of the BPP system is to provide a comprehensive, community-driven platform to all types of cyclists. 
The system acts as an intermediary, processing heterogeneous data inputs from various sources, including external providers, and synthesizing this information to generate reliable output for all users. The users who can interact with the platform are: registered users who provide data by tracking their trips and unregistered users who can only browse and search for paths. \\
The system offers its users a dual mode of use which addresses two different kinds of needs: tracking cycling sessions and both adding and accessing directly acquired information about the status of bike paths. The availability of these modes of use depends on the category the user belongs to. \\
The first mode of use is reserved to registered users and it serves as a way to keep a private log of their training sessions and to obtain and store personal performance metrics for each session, together with meteorological information retrieved from external sources, when it is available. \\ The second mode is for both registered and external users and it allows them to access an active inventory of paths which can be visualized on a map and which contain information about their status and their effectiveness. \\ These two modes are completely independent and only have one point of contact: registered users who have tracked a cycling session can decide to publish only the geographical tracking of the path they have ridden along and two types of information about it: an indication of its status and a specification of whether relevant obstacles are present along the way. \\
Data publication by registered users is allowed in only two distinct modes: \textbf{manual mode}, where users manually input the names of the street that compose the path and their status and \textbf{automated mode}, where the system automatically acquires data from the user's mobile device's  GPS, accelerometer and gyroscope to reconstruct the path and to detect significant movements that might be indicative of the presence of obstacles. For both reliability and quality control, when automated mode is used, the user must confirm the elaborated data to make it publishable. \\


\subsubsection{World Phenomena}
\begin{itemize}
    \item WP1: Bikers ride along paths. 
    \item WP2: The bike’s speed changes over time. 
    \item WP3: Bikers track their training sessions with a mobile device that is equipped with the following sensors: GPS, accelerometer and gyroscope. 
    \item WP4: Every path has an origin and a destination. 
    \item WP5: Every path is made of one or more streets. 
    \item WP6: Given the set of streets that compose it, a path is a way of crossing all and only those streets.
    \item WP7: Every path may have obstacles.
    \item WP8: Bikers have a subjective opinion and evaluation of the path’s conditions based on their direct experience.
    \item WP9: Bikers consider each other reliable when it comes to recommending paths.
    \item WP10: GPSs, accelerometers and gyroscopes can perform inaccurate readings.
    \item WP11: The bikers’ coordinates change as they move along a path.
    \item WP12: Meteorological conditions are strictly linked to a specific location and time.
    \item WP13: Bikers actively observe and collect information regarding the path as they travel along it. 
\end{itemize}


\subsubsection{Shared Phenomena}
\begin{itemize}
    \item SP1: A registered user starts or stops tracking a session on the application.
    \item SP2: Raw sensor data provided by the GPS, accelerometer and gyroscope is actively transmitted and received by the system during the tracking of a session.
    \item SP3: The system provides all the paths that have been published.
    \item SP4: The system provides the total distance for each published path.
    \item SP5: The system provides performance metrics only to the user who is the owner of the path.
    \item SP6: The system may provide meteorological information only to the user who is the owner of the path.
    \item SP7: The system shows the score for each published path.
    \item SP8: Bikers are provided with a selection of published paths that connect their specified origin and destination, if such published paths exist in the system.
    \item SP9: Users grant the system access to meteorological data from external sources on their device.
    \item SP10: The system allows insertion of publishable paths from the user only in two modes: manual mode and automated mode.
    \item SP11: In manual mode, the user manually inputs the following path data: crossed streets names, status and obstacles, starting point and ending point into the system.
    \item SP12: In automated mode, the user confirms or corrects the information that has been automatically acquired by the system.
    \item SP13: A publishable path is a path that has been manually inserted or automatically registered and then confirmed by the owner of the path.
    \item SP14: The system shows bike paths between an origin and a destination specified by the user ordered by their score, if they exist.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Definitions, Acronyms, Abbreviations}
\subsubsection{Definitions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Global Positioning System}: a satellite-based navigation system owned by the U.S. government that uses a constellation of orbiting satellites, ground control stations, and receivers to provide precise positioning, navigation, and timing information.
    \item \textbf{Accelerometer}: a sensor that measures linear acceleration (changes in velocity over time) along one or more axes.
    \item \textbf{Gyroscope}: a sensor that measures angular velocity. It detects how fast an object is rotating around a specific axis, which is essential for determining a device's current orientation in space.
    \item \textbf{Meteorological Service Provider}: is a formal entity that is responsible for the collection, processing, analysis, forecasting, and dissemination of meteorological and related environmental information and services.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Scenarios}

\textbf{Registered user tracks a training session in automated mode} \\
Registered user RU wants to track a training session and add it to their personal archive. RU enters the BPP platform with their personal email and password and, once they are logged in, they open the menu and access the "Track my ride" function. The system then asks RU to specify whether they want to use manual or automated mode; RU selects automated mode. At this point, if the system has not been authorized before, it asks RU to grant it access to the mobile device's GPS, accelerometer and gyroscope. Once the system is able to retrieve this data, it signals RU that it is ready to record the session. RU can now start cycling and BPP will take care of elaborating the incoming flow of data to detect when the activity has started and when it is over and to generate an informative record of the session. Once either the system detects that the activity is over or RU signals it is, the system displays an overview of the navigated path and forces RU to confirm or discard status and obstacle information that it has automatically computed before letting RU save the session. Once the user has checked all the information, the record of the activity, enriched with human readable performance metrics and meteorological information (if it is available), is automatically saved and accessible to RU.

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Registered user visualizes a previous training session} \\
Registered User RU wants to visualize a previous training session. RU enters the BPP platform with their personal email and password and, once they are logged in, they open the menu and access the "View my rides" function. The system then visualizes the log of sessions that RU has recorded and allows them to change the visualization order based on the available data (e.g. chronological order, ordered by the length of the sessions etc.). RU finds the session they wanted to visualize and opens it; the system reacts by displaying the path they have ridden along on the map and the associated performance metrics and meteorological data (if present).

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Registered user manually inserts a path} \\
Registered user RU has cycled along a path and wants to upload it on BPP to make it available to other bikers. RU enters the BPP platform with their personal email and password and, once they are logged in, they open the menu and access the "Track my ride" function. The system then asks RU to specify whether they want to use manual or automated mode; RU selects manual mode. The system now allows RU to insert the name of the streets that they have crossed one by one and, at each insertion, it visualizes the street on a map and waits for the user to confirm the address or to correct it. Once RU confirms, the system allows them to select the exact segment of the street that they have ridden along directly on the map and it then shows the result of the selection and waits again for RU's confirmation. Once the street or a segment of it has been correctly selected and confirmed, BPP allows RU to insert information about its status. The reconstruction of the path ends when RU signals they are done. BPP takes care of checking whether the inserted streets actually compose a path and of asking RU to start over with the reconstruction in case they do not. Once the path is validated, RU is shown an overview of it and is given the option to either save the path or discard it. 

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Registered user publishes a path} \\
Registered user RU wants to publish a path to make it available to other users. RU enters the BPP platform with their personal email and password and, once they are logged in, they only have two ways of publishing information: they can either publish an already existent path that is saved on their personal archive or they can create a new one through manual or automated mode and then publish it. In either case, the system will only publish the geographical track of the path, its publication date, RU's username and the status of each street and the obstacles that might appear along it. BPP will also assign it a score that it computes by only using this particular data.

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent
\textbf{User visualizes a path}\\
User U wants to find a path that goes from point A to point B. U enters the BPP platform with or without logging in. U then accesses the "Find a path" function from the menu and the system asks them to insert the addresses of their origin and their destination. After each single insertion, BPP visualizes the point on a map and asks U for confirmation or correction. Once both the origin and the destination have been confirmed by U, the system tries to retrieve the existent paths that connect those points. If no suitable path exists, BPP reports an error to U and terminates this workflow; otherwise the system visualizes the best paths, according to their scores, on a map. The number of paths that are shown on the map at the same time is initially set to a default amount that can be modified by U, as far as it does not exceed a fixed upper bound. This upper limit is set by the system in order to achieve a reasonable trade-off between richness and completeness of the information and clearness and neatness of the visualization. U can now select one of the available paths and visualize its details.

\subsection{User Characteristics}
BPP's target users can generally be placed in one of the following user groups: 
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Amateur bikers}: these are more experienced bikers whose main purpose when cycling is training. 
    \item \textbf{Commuters and beginner bikers}: these users cycle mainly as a way to move around the city in their daily life (e.g. daily commute to the work place/university etc.).
    \item \textbf{Casual users}: these are users who are not interested in giving their active contribution; they just want to browse around and might want to access specific information.
\end{itemize}
These different categories of users use the platform for very different reasons and purposes, which means that they will likely be more interested in exploiting certain functionalities more than others. Here is a more detailed categorization of the user groups that have only been briefly defined above. Note that we will not go into details for the casual users who are not intended to be direct stakeholders for BPP.

\subsubsection{Amateur bikers}
\textbf{Users profile}\\
Amateur bikers are experienced bikers who have a strong passion for the sport. They train multiple times per week, have a deeper knowledge and understanding of technical aspects liked to the sport (specific terminology, biomechanical aspects, brand preferences etc.) and they likely own more sophisticated equipment. These users are also more likely to be interested in actively contributing to the bikers community as they directly have to deal with the difficulty of finding proper paths that satisfy their training needs. 

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Educational level}\\
The general educational level of these individuals is not relevant to the use of the system but we assume that they have at least an intermediate level of domain-literacy. More specifically, we assume they are familiar with performance metrics that are commonly used in cycling such as average speed, elevation gain, cadence and moving time.

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Experience with the system}\\
These users will likely use BPP almost on a daily basis, therefore, after overcoming the initial learning curve, they are expected to be proficient in the use of the platform and to maintain that proficiency overtime. 

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Technical expertise}\\
Amateur bikers are used to tracking their sessions with mobile tracking devices that are frequently used in cycling such as smartwatches, heart rate monitors, cadence monitors and GPS. The consequent assumption is that these users own some kind of mobile device that has at least a built in GPS, accelerometer and gyroscope and that they know how to use it.


\subsubsection{Commuters and beginner bikers}
\textbf{Users profile}\\
This category of users includes both beginner bikers who are new to the cycling world and commuters, who cycle almost daily as a way to get from one place to the other (e.g. to go to work or university). Both these kinds of cyclists might not always want to track their sessions since especially commuters are not interested in receiving performance metrics but only in paths memorization and discovery. These users usually do not own sophisticated gear besides their mobile phone and, in the average case, they will exploit contributions from other users more than what they will actively contribute.

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Educational level}\\
The general educational level of these individuals is not relevant to the use of the system but we assume that they have very little domain-literacy besides what can be considered common and empirical knowledge outside of the cycling world (e.g. notions of length of a path and capability to evaluate the quality of one).

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Experience with the system}\\
These users will likely not use BPP on a daily basis which might slow down the process to become proficient.

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Technical expertise}\\
These users do not generally have any specific technical expertise beyond the daily use of a mobile phone.

\subsubsection{Note on disabilities}
The intended user groups include cyclists with a wide range of physical and sensory capabilities. The user population is expected to include individuals with audio and visual impairments, specifically color vision deficiencies (CVD) and reduced visual acuity. Furthermore, users may possess motor control limitations, either as permanent physical conditions or as situational constraints induced by the wearing of protective gear (e.g., thick gloves) and the vibration inherent to the cycling environment. These characteristics necessitate accommodations in the subsequent requirements regarding interface contrast, tactile feedback, and audio volume levels.
